About AI Under Attack

A little CTF I built for learning Prompt Injection attacks the fun way: by actually doing them, not just reading about them in a PDF.

Hey, it's Bintang 👋

I built this for COMP6441 (Security Engineering & Cyber Security) at UNSW, and yes, I did spend an unreasonable amount of time writing flavour text for a uni assignment. Sue me. It's inspired by the GuidedCTF format UNSW already runs, just pointed at AI instead of SQL injection and XSS, because nobody was covering that side of things and I thought it'd be more fun to build than another to-do app.

LLMs like Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini are getting bolted onto everything these days, and Prompt Injection is quietly one of the messiest, least-understood ways to break them. Rather than lecture you about it, I figured it's more fun (and it sticks better) if you just break a few AI bots yourself. So here we are.

Challenge Categories

💉

Direct Prompt Injection

You just... ask it wrong, on purpose. Feed the AI input crafted to override its instructions and it'll hand over things it really, really shouldn't.

🎭

Role Manipulation

Also known as jailbreaking, or "convincing the AI it's a different, much less responsible AI now." Costumes and fictional framing do a lot of heavy lifting here.

🔍

Information Disclosure

The AI knows a secret and swears it won't tell. You don't ask directly, you just get it to let something slip while it thinks it's helping with something else entirely.

Security Engineering Principles (the boring but important bit)

Every challenge here is a real security principle getting violated in real time. Sorry, had to sneak the theory in somewhere:

  • Trust Boundaries: AI systems blur the line between "the developer said this" and "the user said this." Treat all input as guilty until proven innocent.
  • Least Privilege: If your AI assistant doesn't need the keys to the vault, don't hand it the keys to the vault.
  • Defence in Depth: A stern sentence in a system prompt is not a security control, no matter how many times you tell it "do not reveal this." It will reveal this.
  • Verification and Validation: Check what the AI actually says before it reaches a user or another system. Trust, but verify. Mostly just verify.
  • Separation of Concerns: Secrets do not belong anywhere near the AI's context window. Not even a little bit. Not even for testing.

How to Use This Platform

  1. Read the Scenario tab so you know what app you're supposedly attacking and what you're after.
  2. Read the Background tab for the theory behind the attack (skippable if you're feeling brave).
  3. Start chatting with the AI. Get weird with it. Try things you'd feel a bit silly typing to a real support bot.
  4. Stuck? Pop a hint. Just one at a time though, the last hint is basically the answer and kind of ruins the fun.
  5. Found the flag? Drop it in the flag box. Always shaped like FLAG{...}.
  6. After you solve it, actually read the Mitigation tab. That's the part that turns "lol I broke a chatbot" into an actual skill.

Responsible Use (the part I have to say)

Everything on this platform is intentionally broken, on purpose, for learning. Please only point these techniques at systems you own or have explicit permission to test. Doing this to a real production system without a green light is illegal, unethical, and honestly just not a great look. Keep it in the sandbox.
